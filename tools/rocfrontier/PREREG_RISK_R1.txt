WHY THIS AND NOT ANOTHER NEW LEG (the reasoning, from the corpus - RESEARCH_LEDGER, BOOK.md 10a-10r):
- #463's score is set by two drawdowns: WF = the March 2020 crash ($44,849 valued daily), LB = one ENGU-Q open-profit give-back
  (2026-06-18..26, $49,855). Every single-seat swap moved one of them, never both (10q).
- What survived kept every trade and re-sized it (NOISE #422's squeeze tilt; V2). What died removed trades (every filter: they cut
  positive-expectancy trades / the ENGU-Q refill effect), changed exits (closed on every leg), added legs (each added drawdown faster than
  return; at #463's Sortino ~3.8 a zero-correlation leg needs Sortino ~1.2 just to lift the book 5%), or learned per-trade sizes (none beat
  its own size-shuffle null in both stretches - docs/ML_SHUFFLE_AUDIT_2026-09-30.md).
- V2 is the only structural change that beat #463 in BOTH stretches: WF 116.1 vs 92.7, LB 259.3 vs 164.8 %/yr at $30k (10o, old
  convention). It was parked as a 12-month forward shadow (VT) that only counts if a >$20k drawdown happens, because its whole gain is
  those two drawdowns and it had the better net/DD in only 7 of 15 years.
- Theory says that is the signature to expect, not a reason to doubt it: if volatility clusters and the book's edge per unit of risk does
  not rise with volatility (trades V2 sizes up vs down: PF 1.46 vs 1.43), equal risk through time is the better allocation and its benefit
  shows up as shallower drawdowns, not as more years won (Moreira & Muir 2017; Barroso & Santa-Clara 2015). Against it: Cederburg,
  O'Doherty, Wang & Yan (2020, JFE) find real-time vol-managed versions of ~100 strategies do not beat the unmanaged ones in general.
- The ML question: every ML failure here was on sparse, noisy trade-outcome labels or on learned per-trade sizes. Risk is the one target
  with a dense label (every day) and strong persistence, so if ML earns a role on this book it is forecasting RISK for the one sizing
  mechanism that works. R5 below asks that once, with a falsifiable bar, instead of a model bake-off.
- Nothing on this question was ever tested: no timing null on V2, no drawdown test beyond the worst one, no spanning regression, and the
  engine could not run a sized book. The engine feature now exists (augur_engine/book_sizing.py, book_sizing block on BOOK jobs).

STATISTICS (scale-free; x = the stretch's daily valued P&L, one value per row, zeros included)
- ROC  = 30 x (net / years) / maxDD; drawdown from a peak that starts at 0; years = (last row - first row) / 365.25.
- SORT = mean(x) / sqrt(mean(min(x, 0)^2)) x sqrt(252).
- CDR  = 30 x (net / years) / CDaR95, where CDaR95 = the mean of the largest ceil(5% x rows) daily drawdown values
         dd_t = max(0, running peak from 0 - cum_t) (Chekhlov, Uryasev & Zabarankin 2005): the path statistic that sees every bad stretch.
- UPI  = (net / years) / sqrt(mean(dd_t^2)) (Martin's Ulcer performance index) - reported only.

PRIMARY TESTS (the primary cell is V2; every threshold is fixed here)
E1 IS* read (partly seen - above). PASS = V2 beats RAW on ROC AND SORT AND CDR, on rule U AND on rule S.
E2 Timing null, family-aware. T = max over {V2, V2-500, V2-60} of (CDR of the cell - CDR of RAW) on the stretch.
   Null A: every circular shift k of the multiplier rows of all three cells over the pre-lockbox rows (2010-06-07..2025-06-29),
           k in [250, N - 250], the same k for every cell; each shifted multiplier re-sizes every trade by its entry row (a trade-level
           rebuild - closed dollars and every daily mark - not a scaling of daily P&L).
   Null B: 999 stationary-bootstrap resamples (Politis & Romano 1994) of the same multiplier rows, mean block 60 rows, the same resampled
           rows for every cell, numpy default_rng seed 20261003.
   PASS = T(real) >= the 95th percentile of Null A AND of Null B, on WF AND on EX, on rule U AND on rule S (8 conditions).
   Reported beside it: V2's own percentile in each null; the same nulls with ROC instead of CDR.
E3 Equal-net worst drawdowns - several episodes, and V2 is charged for the drawdowns it creates by sizing up in calm tape (58b's failure
   mode). On ALL and on ALLX: scale V2's daily series by c = net(RAW) / net(V2) so both have the same net; split each series into
   underwater periods (maximal runs of rows below its running peak from 0) and take each one's five deepest. PASS = V2's k-th deepest is
   shallower than RAW's k-th deepest for at least 4 of k = 1..5 AND the sum of V2's five is smaller - on ALL AND ALLX, on rule U AND S.
   Reported with it: the dates and depth of every one of those drawdowns for both books (V2's own $35,304 WF episode is named this way
   before the verdict is read), and for each of V2's five: the dollars lost inside it by ENGU-Q positions entered at m > 1 and still open
   at its peak (the 58b mechanism).

- Power check: in a synthetic world with a PLANTED volatility mechanism (the edge per unit of risk turns negative at extreme volatility),
  V2 halved the five deepest drawdowns at equal net and cleared E1 and E3 under both day clocks, yet E2's family-aware timing null sat at the
  93rd-96th percentile, so the AND over two clocks (8 conditions) failed it on one. In the NULL synthetic world (a constant edge per unit
  of risk) E2 sat at the 18th-31st percentile. Fifteen years hold only a couple of dozen independent volatility regimes, so a timing null
  cannot be sharper than that; requiring it on two clocks doubles the chance of failing by noise.
- Each clock is internally causal (index and entry stamps share one rule; tests/test_book_sizing*.py prove the multiplier for a day never
  reads that day), so the second clock adds noise, not protection, to a TIMING test.
