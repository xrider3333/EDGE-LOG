WHAT IS DIFFERENT (one line): the first EL family that ranks stocks on their reaction to a single NEWS EVENT (a day of 3x or 5x
normal volume, typically an earnings release) rather than on a price trend or a calendar - the earnings-announcement-return
drift of Brandt, Kishore, Santa-Clara & Venkatachalam (2008) and the news-continuation result of Chan (2003).

WHY NOW (house evidence first). RESMOM r1 (ledger 2.65) showed that in 2016-25 US large caps, monthly cross-sectional
continuation survives a 10 bps round trip (+$234k / 9 yrs) and - the part that matters - earns in #463's drawdown days (DO 0.160,
above all 500 random books), enough to lift #463's WF from 93.8 to 120.8 as a book add. The intraday version of event
continuation is dead (XGAP r1: no drift from 09:35 to the close). The open question is the MONTHLY horizon after a news event:
whether the market under-reacts to a firm's own news for weeks, and whether that drift, like RESMOM's, lands in the book's
drawdown weeks.

THE MAP FIRST (MDL r1). A dollar-neutral stock basket; rho_dd likely near zero (RESMOM: +0.08). Its standalone bar is the
half-the-time line, ROC @ $30k >= 15; RESMOM showed a cell can fail that bar and still pass the book-add report, and the house
route for that case is MANAGER #70's (the gate against the random-name null, then one forward book line). Placed honestly: a
RESMOM-like cell would sit near ROC 7; the family is worth the round only for its drawdown-day profile.

MECHANISM. Investors under-react to firm-specific news, most of all to earnings, so prices keep drifting in the direction of the
news for weeks (Bernard & Thomas 1989; Chan, Jegadeesh & Lakonishok 1996). Brandt et al. (2008) show the drift is captured by the
market's own reaction - the abnormal return around the announcement - without the earnings numbers, and Chan (2003) finds that
large moves WITH news continue while large moves without news reverse. Volume is the news proxy here (no earnings calendar is
needed: a day of several times normal volume is, for a large cap, almost always an earnings release, guidance or a deal).
Counter-evidence written in now: the earnings drift has shrunk in large caps since the mid-2000s and may be gone (Chordia,
Subrahmanyam & Tong 2014; Martineau 2021, "Rest in peace post-earnings announcement drift"); the reaction-day signal overlaps
12-1 momentum (RESMOM's) - the correlation is reported and, if both lines run, the owner hears ONE finding on continuation.
