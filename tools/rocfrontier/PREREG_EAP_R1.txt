WHAT IS DIFFERENT (one line): the first EL stock family that is LONG A SCHEDULE - names are bought because a release is due,
never because of what a price, a filing or other traders did - and both sides are the same kind of company.

WHY NOW (house evidence first). The lesson of 2026-10-06 (ledger finding line): every short-growth basket this lane built
(NETISS, SHORTINT, NEWISSUE) earned inside #463's drawdowns and lost it back in the 2019-21 growth rally; the matched-drawdown
yardstick rejects that trade. EAP holds no characteristic tilt by construction: in any month the long side (names due to
report) and the short side (names not due) are drawn from the same Nasdaq-100 pool, and which side a company sits on rotates
with its fiscal calendar, not with its style. EDRIFT r1 died on event-rate costs; this family's primary cell trades MONTHLY
(RESMOM's turnover, which survives costs), and its event-window cell is the one honest event-rate shot, judged by the same bars.

THE MAP FIRST (MDL r1). A dollar-neutral (cell M) and an ES-hedged (cell W) basket, placed as a standalone leg first and a
drawdown-set candidate second. Standalone bar: ROC @ $30k >= 15. Placed honestly: Frazzini & Lamont (2007) report about 7%
a year for announcers minus non-announcers across US stocks; in 100 mega-caps a few percent is plausible - at $200,000 a side
~$4,000-10,000 a year against a drawdown of perhaps $15,000-30,000 (ROC 3-15). Savor & Wilson (2016) read the premium as pay
for MARKET risk revealed at announcements - so its drawdown-day profile may be the wrong sign; that is printed first, not hoped
away.

MECHANISM. Announcers earn more than non-announcers in the weeks around a scheduled release: attention-driven buying by
individual investors (Frazzini & Lamont 2007; Barber & Odean 2008), compensation for the market-wide news that announcements
carry (Savor & Wilson 2016), and demand for the anticipated volatility (Johnson & So 2018). Barber, De George, Lehavy & Trueman
(2013) find it in most countries, concentrated in the days around the release. Counter-evidence written in now: (1) the
premium is weaker in large, liquid names; (2) a predicted date can miss the actual one by a week (companies move their
calendars); (3) Nasdaq-100 coverage only: ~100 names a rank, so 50 a side is impossible - sides are sized by dollars, not
counts (below); (4) the 2016-25 sample has one growth boom and bust, but here both sides are Nasdaq-100 names.

CELLS (the family: 2).
- M (MONTHLY, the Frazzini-Lamont rule). At each month's last session close r, among the universe names: LONG the names DUE
  in the next month (a predicted date in the sessions r+1 .. the next month's last session), SHORT the names NOT due. Each
  side gets $200,000 split equally among its names (CHOICE: sides sized by dollars, because the counts swing with the
  reporting season - about 75 due in January / April / July / October, about 10 in the months between); a month with fewer
  than 8 names on either side trades nothing (counted). FILL at the next session's official open, HOLD to the next
  rebalance's fill (RESMOM's engine, the per-name size the side's dollars over its count).
- W (EVENT WINDOW). Each predicted date p of a universe name (universe taken at the rank before the entry): LONG $4,000 from
  the official open of the 3rd session before p to the official open of the 2nd session after p (5 sessions, covering a
  release on p before the open or after the close), HEDGED with ES by the name's ex-ante beta (OLS with an intercept over the
  252 sessions before the entry, zero before 252 exist; DIVRUN r1's hedge, 0.5 bps a side on the hedge); positions overlap
  freely (no cap; the count per session is printed).
