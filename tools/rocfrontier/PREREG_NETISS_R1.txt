WHAT IS DIFFERENT (one line): the first EL family whose signal comes from the firm's own FILINGS - how many shares it has
issued or bought back over a year, read from SEC XBRL facts - not from prices, volumes or a corporate-actions calendar.

WHY NOW (house evidence first). RESMOM r1 showed that a monthly 50-long / 50-short basket of the 500 most liquid US stocks
survives a 10 bps round trip and that WHERE a leg earns decides its book value: RES earned in #463's drawdown days (DO 0.160,
above all 500 random books), enough to lift #463's WF from 93.8 to 120.8. DIVRUN r1 (same day) showed the other side of the
house rule: a real but small event edge (+28 bps a quarter) dies on costs at event turnover. Net issuance is a slow
characteristic (a firm's share count moves a few percent a year), so a monthly sort on it turns over little, and its short side
- firms that keep issuing stock, mostly through stock compensation and stock-paid deals - is the growth corner that falls
hardest in the sell-offs that make #463's drawdown weeks.

THE MAP FIRST (MDL r1). A dollar-neutral basket; the mechanism predicts it EARNS in growth sell-offs (short issuers), so it is
placed as a drawdown-week candidate first and a standalone leg second. Its standalone bar is the half-the-time line, ROC @ $30k
>= 15; the house route for a cell that fails it but earns in the book's drawdowns is MANAGER #70's (a gate against the
random-name null, then at most one forward book line), judged INCREMENTALLY over #463 + 0.264 x RES. Placed honestly: the
literature's long-short spread is a few percent a year in large caps; at $200,000 a side that is ~$6,000 a year against a
drawdown of perhaps $20,000-40,000 (ROC 5-9) - the family is worth the round only for its drawdown-day profile.

MECHANISM. Firms issue stock when it is expensive and buy it back when it is cheap, and investors under-react to both
(Ikenberry, Lakonishok & Vermaelen 1995; Loughran & Ritter 1995); the 12-month change in split-adjusted shares outstanding
predicts returns with a negative sign in every size group, the largest included (Pontiff & Woodgate 2008; Fama & French 2008,
'pervasive'), and the composite issuance measure of Daniel & Titman (2006) adds the stock-paid acquisitions (Loughran & Vijh
1997). It is one of the anomalies that replicate out of sample in modern data (Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen 2023). Counter-evidence
written in now: much of the issuance in 2016-25 large caps is stock compensation at growth firms that kept rising until 2021 -
the short side can lose for years in a growth boom; buybacks became so common that the 'shrinker' tail may be crowded; and the
effect may be mostly in small stocks outside this universe.

SHARES KNOWN AT A RANK CLOSE r (point in time). For a name and a concept, a fact is usable at r when its FIRST filed date (the
earliest filing that carries that value for that end date) is strictly before r's date (a filing accepted after the close on
r's own date is not yet tradable). S(r) = the usable value with the latest end date (its as-of date a = end); amendments never
replace a value already usable. CONCEPT ORDER per name and rank: dei:EntityCommonStockSharesOutstanding (the cover page,
non-dimensional), else us-gaap:CommonStockSharesOutstanding; the two values of one score always come from the SAME concept
(never mixed); the weighted-average concept is a reported cross-check only.

SCORE. ISS_k(r) = ln( S(r) x F(a) / ( S' x F(a') ) ), where S' is the usable value (same concept, filed before r) whose as-of
date a' is closest to a - k years inside a +-35-day band (k = 1 or 2), and F is the SIPORB cache's split factor on the as-of
date (the previous session when the as-of date is not one) - so a split between a' and a cancels and a real issuance or buyback
remains. A name has NO score when: either value is missing or not positive; a' falls before the cache's first session
2016-01-04 (F unknown - no split history is held before it; the first ranks are therefore partly unscored, counted by rank);
|ISS| > ln(10) (a unit or scale error in a filing, or a merger that remade the firm - counted and listed by name, never kept).
