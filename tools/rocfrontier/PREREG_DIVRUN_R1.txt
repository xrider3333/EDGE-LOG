MECHANISM. Investors treat dividends as free income (Hartzmark & Solomon 2019, "The dividend disconnect"), so demand for a
stock rises as its dividend nears: stocks earn abnormal returns of about 40 bps in the months they are predicted to pay (the
dividend month premium, Hartzmark & Solomon 2013, JFE; 1927-2011, large caps included), which they trace to that demand rather
than to risk. The two windows (10 and 20 sessions before the predicted ex-date) bet that the pressure builds into the last
cum-dividend close; selling at that close keeps the run-up and skips the ex-day drop and the dividend itself (no tax or ex-day
arithmetic in the P&L). Where in the month the premium actually sits is measured, not assumed (the event-time path is
reported). Counter-evidence written in now: the premium was measured mostly before 2012; demand for dividends was strongest
when rates were near zero (2016-21) and may be weaker in 2022-25; large caps are the most arbitraged names; and the premium may
sit earlier in the month, or after the ex-day, outside both windows. The index-level cousin (market returns on days of big
aggregate dividend PAYMENTS - reinvestment flows) is ES on daily bars, TV's lane, and is routed there as an idea, not tested
here.
