WHAT IS DIFFERENT (one line): the signal is the CASH a firm paid to (or raised from) its shareholders over the last four
quarters, read from the cash-flow statement in its SEC filings and divided by its market value - NETISS counts SHARES; the two
part ways wherever buybacks only offset stock compensation (cash out, share count flat) and wherever shares are issued for no
cash (stock pay, stock-paid deals).

WHY NOW (house evidence first). RESMOM r1 (ledger 2.65) showed that a monthly 50-long / 50-short basket of the 500 most liquid
US stocks survives costs and that a leg's value is decided by WHERE it earns: RES earned in #463's drawdown days and lifted the
book's WF from 93.8 to 120.8. MANAGER #100: the only structural improvement left is a new leg that earns on the reference
line's residual drawdown set (45 episodes / 762 days). Firms that hand cash back are mature and profitable; firms that raise
cash from shareholders are the young, cash-burning growth corner that falls hardest in the sell-offs behind #463's drawdown
weeks - the drawdown-week story of NETISS, measured in dollars instead of shares.

THE MAP FIRST (MDL r1). A dollar-neutral basket; the mechanism predicts it EARNS in growth sell-offs (short the raisers, long
the payers), so it is placed as a drawdown-week candidate first and a standalone leg second. Standalone bar ROC @ $30k >= 15;
the route for a cell that fails it but earns in the book's drawdowns is MANAGER #70's (a gate against the random-name null,
then at most one forward book line), judged INCREMENTALLY over the reference line. Placed honestly: the literature's spread is
a few percent a year and smaller among the largest stocks; at $200,000 a side that is ~$4,000-8,000 a year (ROC 4-8 at a
$30,000 drawdown) - the family is worth the round only for its drawdown-day profile, and its overlap with NETISS is the first
risk.

MECHANISM. Net payout yield - dividends plus repurchases less issuance, over market value - predicts returns better than the
dividend yield alone, because since the 1980s firms pay out through buybacks (Boudoukh, Michaely, Richardson & Roberts 2007;
Grullon & Michaely 2002); firms that announce open-market repurchases outperform for years (Ikenberry, Lakonishok & Vermaelen
1995; Peyer & Vermaelen 2009) and firms that raise equity underperform (Loughran & Ritter 1995) - managers time the market in
their own stock and investors under-react. Counter-evidence written in now: payout yield is close to a VALUE sort, and value
lost to growth through most of 2017-2020; 2016-25's biggest buyback programmes sit at the largest growth firms, so the long
side is not purely defensive; much large-cap buyback spending only offsets stock compensation, so a high cash yield need not
mean a shrinking share count; and the effect is documented mostly in broad samples, not the 500 largest names.

CASH KNOWN AT A RANK CLOSE r (point in time, NETISS [A2]): a fact (concept, start, end) is usable at r when the first session
after its FIRST filed date is on or before r; its value is the value in that first filing (amendments never replace a usable
value). Period types by length: Q 77-105 days, H 168-196, 9M 259-287, FY 350-380. The STATEMENT the score reads is the latest
usable period ending e that is a FY or a year-to-date period (start = the fiscal year's start s) with a usable STATEMENT fact.
TRAILING FOUR QUARTERS: if that period is a FY, TTM = its value; else TTM = YTD(s .. e) + FY_prev - YTD_prev, where FY_prev is
the usable FY ending within 14 days before s and YTD_prev the usable period of the same type ending within 14 days of e - 1
year (each must have its STATEMENT fact). Each component is read on exactly those periods, period by period: its first concept
that has a fact for the period, else its fallback; a component with neither on a period whose STATEMENT fact exists reads ZERO
for that period (the line is not on the statement: no buyback, no dividend, no issue - a firm that starts or stops buying back
inside the window is the common case). REP, DIV and ISS are the components' TTMs (all >= 0; a negative fact is unusable).

MARKET VALUE MV(r) = raw close(r) x S x F(a) / F(r), where S is NETISS's S(r) (its concept order, as-of date a) and the factor
carries a split between a and r into the share count; the window a .. r is split-checked both ways against the calendar exactly
as NETISS [A3] / [A9] (a miss voids the score).

SCORES and CELLS (the family: 2), at each month's last session close r:

- R (net repurchase yield - the buyback channel without dividends, so the cell is not a dividend-yield sort): R = (REP - ISS) /
  MV(r).
