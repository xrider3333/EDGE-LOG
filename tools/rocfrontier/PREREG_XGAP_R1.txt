MECHANISM. Berkman, Koch, Tuttle & Zhang (2012, JFQA, "Paying Attention: Overnight Returns and the Hidden Cost of Buying at the
Open"): attention-driven orders at the open push prices past fair value and the move reverses during the session; it is the
FIRM-SPECIFIC part of the gap that overshoots. House evidence on the index part: NQ gap fade AND gap continuation cleared 0 of
40 cells (ledger 2.5; GAPGO dead) - the brief's "GAPGO alive" is corrected here. ATTN r1 (2026-10-05, the overnight side of
the same paper on SIPORB's stocks in play) died at Stage A: the overnight premium was +7 bps a name-night, under a 10 bps round
trip. The firm-specific intraday axis is untested in house data. Counter-evidence written in before any number: gaps WITH news
(earnings) continue (Chan 2003; post-earnings drift) - the CAP8 cell drops the large gaps where most earnings moves sit; the
member cache has no volume, so no Relative Volume news screen is possible (a named limit, unlike DDW r1's L2).

SIBLING RULE. DDW r1's L2 (no-news gap fade, the top-500 US stocks, ES-beta adjusted, 09:35 -> close) tests a close relative on
a wider universe. If an L2 cell and an XGAP cell both reach Stage B, only the one with the higher A2 book ROC is read on the one
sealed-year day: one look for one hypothesis. Why that is sound [X4]: choosing between the two on the WF stretch and reading the
sealed year once is select-on-WF, test-on-LB - the sealed read is not biased by that choice; only a second look would be, and
the one-day rule forbids it. Both use the same volatility-set c (A2 below), so the comparison is like for like.
