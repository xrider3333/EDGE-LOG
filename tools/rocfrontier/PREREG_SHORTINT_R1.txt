WHAT IS DIFFERENT (one line): the first EL family whose signal is OTHER TRADERS' POSITIONS - the short interest that brokers
report to FINRA twice a month - not prices, volumes, filings or a corporate-actions calendar.

WHY NOW (house evidence first). RESMOM r1 showed that a monthly 50-long / 50-short basket of the 500 most liquid US stocks
survives costs and that WHERE a leg earns decides its book value (RES earned in #463's drawdown days, DO 0.160; the stamp-lag
check of 2026-10-05 confirmed it at weekly resolution, DO 0.173). DIVRUN, EDRIFT and INSIDER (2026-10-05) died for the
opposite reasons: event edges too small for event-rate costs, and no drawdown profile. Days to cover is a slow
characteristic (it moves over weeks), so a monthly sort turns over little; its short side - the names short sellers crowd
into, mostly expensive speculative growth - is the corner that falls hardest when speculation unwinds.

THE MAP FIRST (MDL r1). A dollar-neutral basket placed as a drawdown-set candidate first and a standalone leg second.
Standalone bar: ROC @ $30k >= 15. The house route for a cell that fails it but earns in R: MANAGER #70's (a gate against the
random-name null, then at most one forward book line), judged INCREMENTALLY over L. Placed honestly: Hong, Li, Ni, Scheinkman
& Yan (2015) report a days-to-cover spread near 1% a month across all US stocks, smaller among large caps; in the 500 most
liquid names a few percent a year is plausible - at $200,000 a side ~$6,000-12,000 a year against a drawdown of perhaps
$20,000-40,000 (ROC 5-15). The family is worth the round for its drawdown-set profile.

MECHANISM. Short sellers are informed and short-sale constraints keep their information from being fully priced (Diamond &
Verrecchia 1987; Asquith, Pathak & Ritter 2005; Boehmer, Huszar & Jordan 2010); days to cover - shares sold short over
average daily volume - measures how costly the crowded short is to unwind and is the cleaner signal (Hong et al. 2015), and
the short-interest ratio (shares short over shares outstanding) is the classic one (Dechow, Hutton, Meulbroek & Sloan 2001).
Counter-evidence written in now: (1) much of the short-interest spread is compensation for BORROW FEES on hard-to-borrow
names (Drechsler & Drechsler 2016) - this harness has no fee data, so a binding borrow stress stands in (STAGE A (b)); (2)
SQUEEZES: the 500 most liquid stocks held the 2021 meme names (GME, AMC, BBBY ...), the exact high-days-to-cover shorts, and
a squeeze can take a year's spread in days; (3) the public archive starts 2018-06, so the walk-forward is seven years and
misses 2017 (184 of R's 762 drawdown days); (4) short interest is published with a lag of about two weeks.

DATA - ONE NEW PUBLIC PULL (no keys, no money; run by TV, the house's public-data scout, never by this lane), plus held data.
[D1] FINRA consolidated short interest (TV's catalog row 6): https://cdn.finra.org/equity/otcmarket/biweekly/shrtYYYYMMDD.csv,
one file per settlement date from 2018-06-15 (the archive's first) through the last settlement date whose file is
disseminated before 2025-06-30, every file saved byte for byte with its URL, UTC time, size and sha256 (the photograph rule);
nothing settled or disseminated on or after 2025-06-30 is fetched or read (the sealed year). TV also photographs FINRA's
published short-interest reporting and dissemination schedule if one exists for 2018-25 (it decides [K] below). [D2] TV
extracts ONE flat file (pinned by sha256 in a dated pre-data addendum before any number): settlement date, symbol, issue
name, market, current short interest (shares), previous short interest, FINRA's average daily volume and days to cover (both
REPORTED only), and the file it came from. [D3] held: the SIPORB cache (raw + split-adjusted daily bars, manifest sha256
380b05f2..., never mixed with a re-pull) for prices and for RAW daily share volume; the wide corporate-actions calendar
pinned for RESMOM (sha256 e5bc8487...: dividends [R1], spin-offs [D2] and splits exactly as RESMOM, and its name_change rows
for the symbol map); NETISS r1's pinned as-filed share counts (02387a4f + d10626d8, with their CIK maps) for the
short-interest ratio cell; ES as RESMOM.

SYMBOLS. FINRA files carry the ticker AT the settlement date; the cache carries the current one. A FINRA row joins a cache
name when its symbol equals the name's ticker on that settlement date, built from the calendar's name_change rows (old ->
new, at the process date) walked backward from the cache's ticker; a ticker that two cache names held on overlapping dates,
or a name whose rename precedes the calendar's 2016-06-01 start without TV's hand check, gets NO score (counted and listed).
Share classes follow the file's symbol exactly (GOOG and GOOGL are two names, as in the cache).

UNIVERSE (each rebalance, only information known before the fill): RESMOM's exactly - prior raw close >= $10, the 500 largest
by mean raw dollar volume over the previous 20 sessions, mean volume >= 1,000,000 shares and ATR > $0.50 over the previous 14,
no registered split in the previous 14 sessions, >= 230 split-safe returns and ES pairs in the 252-session window.

[K] KNOWN AT A RANK CLOSE r. A settlement date s is usable at r when the first session after its DISSEMINATION date is on or
before r. The dissemination date is FINRA's published one where TV photographs the schedule; otherwise s + 12 sessions
(firms report two business days after settlement and FINRA publishes within roughly seven to nine sessions of the settlement
date; twelve leaves a margin). At each rank the score uses the LATEST usable settlement s* (counted: its age in sessions at r).

SCORES (one per cell, per name, at s*):
- D1 DAYS TO COVER = SI(s*) / ADV(s*), SI = the file's current short interest, ADV = the mean RAW daily share volume of the
  cache over the 20 sessions ending at s* (the house's own volume, not FINRA's; FINRA's days to cover is a reported
  cross-check). No score when SI is missing or negative, ADV has fewer than 15 sessions, or a split (calendar or F, the
  [A3] two-way rule of NETISS) falls inside the 20 sessions (raw volume would mix share units). SI = 0 is a valid score.
- S1 SHORT-INTEREST RATIO = SI(s*) / S(s*), S = NETISS r1's point-in-time shares outstanding (its [A1]-[A4], [A8] rules:
  first filed value, known at filed + 1 session, cover page else balance sheet, foreign filers excluded) on its as-of date,
  carried to s* by the split factor F (S x F(a) / F(s*)); no score when S is unusable, when its as-of date is more than 200
  days before s*, or when the [A3] split check fails between them. |ln(S1 / median S1 of the rank)| > ln(1000) = a scale
  error: no score, listed.

CELLS (the family: 2). D1 and S1. At each month's last session close r, among the universe names WITH a score: long the 50
LOWEST, short the 50 HIGHEST; a month with fewer than 150 scored names trades the bottom / top third, at least 20 a side, else
nothing (counted). Ties at the cut are broken by the name's ADV, highest first (the cheapest to trade). $4,000 each,
fractional shares, dollar-neutral, FILL at the next session's official open, HOLD to the next rebalance's fill, daily marks,
no compounding - RESMOM's engine unchanged. COSTS: 5 bps a side base, stress 10 and 20.
BORROW: 0.25%/yr on short notional base (RESMOM's), stress 1% and 3%/yr on high-volatility sessions (RESMOM's rule) - and,
because crowded shorts are where borrow fees live, a BINDING flat stress of 3%/yr on every short every day (STAGE A (b)).
Dividends paid and owed exactly as RESMOM [R1].

NULL (family-aware): 500 draws, seed 20261018; at every rebalance each cell's longs and shorts are replaced by the same number
of names drawn at random from that rebalance's eligible SCORED pool (same hygiene, sizing, fills, costs); statistic = the MAX
over the 2 cells of WF ROC @ $30k.
