# EDGELOG Point Score - install (TradingView)

This adds your 9-point score to a TradingView chart. It follows `docs/POINT_SCORE_SPEC.md`.

**Any chart (version 1.3, 2026-10-06):** it works on a 1-minute, 10-second, 5-minute or any other chart, and it always scores **1-minute bars**. Your 10-second pane shows the same score as your 1-minute pane when both panes have the same settings (step 1); a minute's score appears there a moment after that minute closes (on the next 10-second bar). On a chart above 1 minute it shows the last minute of each bar. If you already saved version 1.2, open that script in the Pine Editor, replace its text with the new file and click **Save**.

## Install (3 steps)

1. Open a chart of NQ1! (or MNQ1! / ES1!) in TradingView (1-minute, 10-second or any other timeframe). Open the **Pine Editor** at the bottom, choose **Open > New indicator**, delete the template text, and paste the whole of `POINT_SCORE_1_0.pine`. Click **Save**, name it `EDGELOG Point Score`, then click **Add to chart**. Then turn on TradingView's back-adjustment for the continuous contract (the **B-ADJ** button at the bottom of the chart, also called "Adjust for contracts changes" in the chart settings), so the 30-minute average does not carry the old contract's price gap for about two weeks after every roll; TradingView's help page names no paid-plan requirement, but if the button is missing or locked on your plan, tell MANAGER. Do this on every pane that shows the score: extended hours on, B-ADJ on, and the same indicator Settings; two panes only match when their settings match. If your 10-second chart has no B-ADJ button, its score can differ from the 1-minute pane for about two weeks after each roll.
2. Open the indicator's **Settings**. **Moving average** is **SMA** by default (spec v1.2, your correction of 2026-10-02); switch it to EMA only to compare with the v1.1 scores. The table's ticks are green for a hit, red for a miss and grey for NA; change them with **Hit color**, **Miss color** and **NA color**, and move the table with **Table position** (default top right). **Display** picks the table: **Compact** (default; two rows of coloured cells, MA 10s 1m 5m 30m and Y-L Y-C Y-H Body Vol, with the side, the score and the trend), **Minimal** (one coloured score badge) or **Full** (the detailed table). Bar labels read like `7/9 L`; hover one for the detail. Pick the **Market** (Futures = "today" starts at 09:30, Stocks = 04:00), and set the **Label threshold** (default 7) and **Alert threshold** (default 8). If TradingView shows an error about the 10-second data, your plan has no seconds timeframes: untick **Use 10-second data** (that one point then shows NA).
3. Optional alert: create it from your **1-minute pane**, and on one pane only (on the 10-second pane the same alert comes about 10 seconds later, and on both panes you get every alert twice). Click **Create Alert**, set Condition to `EDGELOG Point Score`, choose **Any alert() function call**, and set it to **Once per bar close**. The message carries the symbol, the 1-minute bar's start time (New York), the side, the score (like 8/9) and which points hit.

## Parity export (only needed once, to check TradingView against EDGE LOG)

Take it from a **1-minute** pane (not the 10-second one). After a few regular sessions have printed on the chart (5 to 10 is plenty, taken after the 15 sessions that follow a futures roll, because the comparison skips those), open the chart menu, choose **Export chart data**, and save the CSV into `C:\EdgeLog\point_score\tv_exports\`. Then tell MANAGER; Claude runs the comparison against EDGE LOG's own numbers. Claude never logs in to TradingView.

## What the table shows

- **Top right, for the last closed 1-minute bar** (on a chart above 1 minute: the last minute of the last closed chart bar): its time, one row per point with a tick, a cross or NA in a Long and a Short column (the side the chart is following is starred), and a Ref column with the EMA or level the close was compared with, or the body and volume it had to beat.
- **Total and trend:** the total for each side as "8/9" (or "7/8 (1 NA)" when a point could not be scored; NA is never counted as a miss), and the daily trend point on its own row, never added into the 9.
- **Bars loaded:** how many bars each timeframe has. A moving-average point stays NA until its timeframe has a full 200-bar window (SMA; the 30-minute one needs about 4-5 trading days) or 600 bars (EMA; about 13 trading days), so it may show "warming up" on a short chart.
