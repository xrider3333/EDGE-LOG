3. THE ONE ROUND-10 CONFIG THAT LOOKED LIKE A WIN  (tools/orb_pyramid_verify.py)
   Add a second unit once a bar CLOSES at +2R, shared stop, one add maximum. It beat the crown on
   EVERY window at an IDENTICAL drawdown (full net $429,220 vs $397,150, PF 1.399 vs 1.371, MAR 0.92
   vs 0.85; 5-year MAR 3.04 vs 2.79; out-of-sample MAR 4.13 vs 3.82), passed gates G1/G2/G3/G5, and
   round 10 rejected it only on the plateau rule. It is legal - the add triggers on a bar CLOSE and
   fills at that close, exactly like the parent's own entry. It still fails, for a better reason:
     * Per-stretch concentration. Strip the five biggest winners from each 200-trade block and the
       pyramid is WORSE than the crown (mean 0.988 vs 0.998, better in only 3 of 11 blocks). Adds
       fire only on trades that already reached +2R, so the whole gain is the tail amplified.
     * Drawdown. Bootstrap says it lowers drawdown in just 20% of resamples (mean +$256 WORSE).
       The identical headline drawdown is one path, not a property.
     * The plateau call was right. Its neighbours are +1.5R (MAR 0.76, clearly worse) and +3.0R
       (MAR 0.84, the knob effectively off). A spike between a worse neighbour and an inert one.

4. POOLING THE ELEVEN ALREADY-VALIDATED CONFIGS  (tools/orb_pool_bootstrap.py)
   Per unit it beat the crown on the full window (MAR 0.90 vs 0.85, drawdown $26,142 vs $28,857,
   96% of the net) and in the out-of-sample year. A year-block bootstrap then killed it: better MAR
   in 28% of resamples, lower drawdown in 33%. The five-year window flatters the crown anyway,
   because 5-year MAR is the exact statistic #314 was crowned on.

5. BAR SIZE / OPENING-RANGE LENGTH  (tools/orb_hunt12_tf.py, ROUND12_tf.txt)
   Neighbours trade 70-90% of the same days at 0.45-0.72 daily correlation. Same bet in a hat.

VERDICT
Across five candidates and eight distinct selectors, NOTHING beats leaving the parameters alone.
Picking on all history returns $166k over the eleven forward years, on recent history $233-279k,
pooling the top-K $182-225k, a config drawn at random $312k, and leaving the parent defaults
untouched $377k. Pooling reliably beats picking - drawdown falls monotonically as the pool grows -
so the ranking is: leave it alone, then pool, then pick. Picking is the worst thing we do.
This does NOT say the strategy is weak. It says our selection step has no forward skill on it, and
the honest ORB programme from here is to freeze the parameters and let the paper legs report.
